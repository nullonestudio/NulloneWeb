Augmented and virtual reality have spent years in the "impressive demo" category. That's changing: headsets are cheaper and lighter, phones have excellent AR built in, and WebXR lets an experience run straight from a link. The question is no longer *can we?* but *where does it pay off?*

Here are the four use cases where we consistently see a return.

## 1. Training and simulation

VR training lets people practise dangerous, expensive or rare situations safely and repeatedly — equipment operation, emergency response, customer conversations. Because every action is tracked, you get objective data on competence instead of a sign-off sheet.

**Measure:** time-to-competency, error rates, training cost per employee.

## 2. Product demos for complex or heavy products

If your product is too big, too expensive or too complicated to bring to a meeting, VR and web 3D can put it in the room at true scale. Prospects can see inside it, run it and understand it far faster than from a brochure.

**Measure:** sales-cycle length, demos per rep, shipping and travel costs saved. See our [Immersive B2B Sales Demo](/work/immersive-b2b-sales-demo) case study.

## 3. Real estate and architecture

Buyers can walk through a home or office before it's built, change finishes and understand the space. Developers sell earlier and reduce the number of physical site visits.

**Measure:** pre-sales conversion, site visits per sale, time on market.

## 4. Retail and e-commerce

AR try-ons and "view in your room" features help customers buy with confidence — which means fewer returns.

**Measure:** conversion rate, return rate, average order value.

## How to scope your first project

1. **Pick one metric.** If you can't name what will improve, don't build it yet.
2. **Choose the lightest platform that works.** WebXR or mobile AR often beat headsets for reach.
3. **Prototype comfort early.** Test on target hardware in week one.
4. **Plan for content updates.** Products change; your experience should too.
